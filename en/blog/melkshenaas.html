Introducing Melkshenas: Technical & Legal Apartment Assessment — Before You Pay

If you're here, you probably know my main line of work: construction management and Lean Construction. Today, though, I want to introduce something different — Melkshenas (melkshenaas.ir), a technical, engineering and legal assessment service for residential apartments in Tehran. The reason I'm featuring it is simple: its philosophy is exactly the principle I've pursued on construction projects for years — decisions should be based on data, not guesswork.

The biggest financial decision of a lifetime, made with the least data

Buying a home is the largest financial transaction most Iranian families ever make, yet the way it's decided is odd: a half-hour visit, the broker's pitch, the freshly renovated looks, and then the contract is signed. Technical and legal information — structural condition, build quality, energy performance, title and permit status — either isn't available at all, or exists but nobody qualified is there to interpret it. Curiously, on large construction projects we commission a technical report for every major decision and never move without documentation; but when buying a home that holds a lifetime of value, most of us decide on intuition. Melkshenas was built precisely to close this gap.

What exactly does Melkshenas do?

Melkshenas (melkshenaas.ir) is a property assessment service designed for residential apartments in Tehran. A technical and engineering expert visits the property and evaluates it in a structured way across technical, engineering and legal dimensions. The output is a comprehensive, readable report stamped and signed by a court-appointed official expert — a credible document that supports the purchase decision, carries weight in price negotiations, and can be cited if a dispute ever arises. That is precisely what an ordinary buyer cannot put together on their own.

The MPR rating model: 120 indicators, six categories

At the heart of Melkshenas is its proprietary rating model, MPR (Model for Property Rating). It examines each property across 120 indicators, grouped into six main categories with defined weights:

  • Location — 25%
  • Legal status — 20%
  • Structure & safety — 15%
  • Facilities — 15%
  • Quality of construction — 15%
  • Energy efficiency — 10%

What matters most to me is that the model's output is a comparable score: you can put two or more candidate properties side by side against one scientific yardstick, rather than choosing whichever one "felt right". Anyone familiar with Lean Construction knows why this matters — when the criteria are transparent, negotiation and decisions become transparent too.

The process is only three steps

Simplicity is one of the first things I noticed about Melkshenas. Step one, register the request: you submit the property's address and details and choose your service level. Step two, the expert visit: a technical expert visits the property and carries out the assessment against the MPR indicators. Step three, receive the report: the comprehensive assessment report — including a final score out of 10, an analysis of strengths and weaknesses, and the expert's recommendations — is delivered to you. No extra complexity: you submit the request, the expert does the technical work, and a decision-ready report arrives.

Three service levels, matching need and budget

Melkshenas offers three service levels, priced relative to the official court-expert tariff: Basic Assessment at half the tariff, the Complete Assessment Report at the full tariff, and Specialized Consultation at 1.5 times the tariff. That means everyone — from someone who simply wants a serious pre-purchase check, to someone who needs a full documented analysis of a specific property — can find an option that fits their need and budget. For exact, up-to-date fees it's best to check the Melkshenas website directly.

Who is this service for?

In my view, three groups are Melkshenas's direct audience. First, buyers looking at apartments in Tehran who don't want such a consequential decision to rest on looks and a broker's word. Second, people torn between several options who want a fair, scientific comparison. Third, sellers who want to document their property's real quality with a credible certificate and stay close to a fair price. If you're in any of these groups, it's worth taking a look at the Melkshenas website before your next serious decision.

Buying or selling an apartment in Tehran?

Before your next move, assess the property with Melkshenas's 120-indicator model — with a report stamped by a court-appointed expert.
